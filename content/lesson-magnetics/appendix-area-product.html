Magnetics · Appendix A — Area product by magnetic kind (draft)

Draft appendix to the magnetics lesson, which the 2025 lectures V8 (inductor design) and V9 (transformer design) cover until the 2026 lesson is written. Numbers come from scripts/area_product_bias.py and are recorded in area-product-checks.json.

V8 sizes an inductor from the stored energy and the window. V9 sizes a transformer by iteration, from the balance of copper loss and core loss. Both methods give an area product . This appendix shows why one flux density cannot serve both.

A size proxy that uses one flux density for every magnetic gives a biased result. It sizes DC-dominated chokes well, but it makes AC-flux magnetics far too small. An audit of six built components showed this pattern. The DC chokes landed within −30 % to +42 % of the real area product. The AC-flux magnetics (an ICT, an ICMCI and a DAB transformer) came out 64 % to 74 % too small.

This appendix explains the cause and gives the fix. Saturation limits the flux in a choke. Core loss limits the flux in an AC-flux magnetic. The two kinds therefore need two sizing methods: the core-geometry constant for chokes, and for AC-flux parts.

At saturation flux, the core cannot get rid of its heat. At 100 kHz and = 300 mT, 3C90 loses 1.24 MW/m³. An ETD54 core (35.5 cm³) would dissipate 44 W. This is the main term of the bias. A proxy that uses = 300 mT for an AC-flux part overstates by about three times. It understates by the same factor.

The window fill adds a second term. A DC choke uses solid round wire, and = 0.4 is a normal value (McLyman, ch. 4). An AC-flux part at 100 kHz needs litz wire or foil to control skin and proximity loss. Litz strands, serving and air between bundles reduce to about 0.25 to 0.30. A transformer with reinforced isolation also needs tape, margins or triple-insulated wire, and falls to about 0.2.

A.6 For KPI owners: the acceptance rule

An area-product KPI that uses one flux density for all magnetics compares parts of different kinds on a biased scale. The fix has three rules:

  1. Report the area product separately for each magnetic kind.
  2. For DC-dominated chokes, keep or at the saturation flux density.
  3. For AC-flux magnetics (ICT, coupled inductors, transformers), use . It sets the flux swing from core loss at the operating frequency.

alone does not remove the bias, because it still sizes the flux at saturation.

Acceptance test. Replay the six components of the earlier proxy audit with the new method. Fit nothing to those six parts. Calculate the bias between kinds as in section A.5. If the bias is below 1.5×, the KPI is qualified. If it is 1.5× or more, report the KPI as unqualified.

The audit gave a bias of 3× to 4× with the old proxy. This appendix predicts that most of the bias comes from , with a smaller part from . If the replay still fails, check first. Litz construction and isolation build-up differ more from part to part than the core loss does.

A.7 What if

  • What if the core size steps? The reference rejects ETD49 for the DAB transformer by 0.3 % of loss (6.47 W against 6.45 W). With a small fan, or with 3 % less current, ETD49 is sufficient and the proxy error becomes −74 %. Catalog steps of 1.5× to 1.8× in limit the resolution of any single-part comparison.
  • What if the voltage is a square wave? Steinmetz data come from sine-wave tests. Square-wave flux with short transitions has more loss. The improved generalized Steinmetz equation (iGSE) corrects this by using only the same three parameters (Venkatachalam et al., 2002).
  • What if a choke has a large ripple? A boundary-mode choke has , so its AC flux is as large as its DC flux. Its core loss is not small. Size it with both and , and use the larger core.
  • What if the DC flux is not zero? A DC bias raises ferrite core loss at the same AC amplitude. An ICT with unequal phase currents has a DC flux, so check its saturation margin as well.
  • What if a powder core is used? Powder cores have a soft saturation and higher core loss than ferrite. Use Mueller's method for a DC-dominated powder choke. For AC flux, use with the Steinmetz data of the powder material.
