The figure holds the other circuit parameters fixed to isolate this effect. As separation becomes comparable with conductor width, fringing weakens the accuracy of the plate approximation.

An intentional reference plane gives a different design tradeoff. Bringing a signal or power conductor closer to its return plane increases its capacitance to that reference and reduces loop inductance. For wide parallel conductors in a uniform medium:

Thus thinner dielectric to a continuous quiet reference is often helpful for field confinement and low-inductance current return. Thinner dielectric directly between an unshielded aggressor and a sensitive victim increases mutual coupling. Always identify which conductors the distance separates. In the ideal uniform TEM model, L′C′ = µε, so changing h alone changes impedance but not propagation velocity; changing dielectric permittivity changes velocity as well.

Distance through insulation, clearance and creepage are separate concepts. Thickness h is a path through solid dielectric; clearance is an air path and creepage follows an insulating surface. A uniform-field estimate is E ≈ ΔV/h: for example, 400 V across 0.10 mm corresponds to 4 kV/mm.

This is a field calculation, not a voltage-withstand rating. Material, manufacturing quality, edge geometry, voltage waveform and the applicable insulation requirements determine a qualified design. No universal safe mm/kV rule follows from the capacitance formula. See TI's explanation of distance through insulation.

Spacing choice. More separation reduces the plate-model coupling capacitance. It also changes the board geometry and stackup. If that change is impractical, compare reducing overlap or edge speed. Recalculate the victim response; injected current alone does not settle the choice.
