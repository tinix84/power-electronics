9.1 Why Laplace as well as Fourier? States, convergence and pole location

Fourier and Laplace describe the same dynamics from different viewpoints; Fourier is not restricted to a single sinusoid or incapable of describing transients. A decaying transient can have an ordinary Fourier transform. Fourier series describe periodic responses, while Fourier integrals describe a wider class of signals.

Initial conditions can also be represented through appropriate equivalent sources. The practical advantage of unilateral Laplace is that initial state enters the transformed differential equations explicitly.

Laplace also introduces the real coordinate σ in s = σ + jω. It supplies exponential weighting and a region of convergence, not just a formal replacement of a derivative by s. For example, exp(at)u(t), with a > 0, has Laplace transform 1/(s − a) for Re(s) > a, but no ordinary Fourier transform.

Substituting jω into that rational expression lies outside its convergence region. It does not turn the growing mode into a bounded sinusoidal steady state. See Swarthmore's Laplace exponential examples.

Poles describe natural exponential modes; zeros depend on how a selected input excites a selected output and can shape phase or produce inverse response. A right-half-plane zero does not by itself make a system unstable (§16.10). For a finite-dimensional continuous-time state model, eigenvalues strictly in the left half-plane imply asymptotic internal stability.

Ideal LC poles on the imaginary axis give bounded free oscillations, but not asymptotic stability or BIBO stability: resonant forcing can grow the response. Repeated imaginary-axis modes need additional care. Hidden modes can disappear from a particular transfer function, so a cancellation must not be used to assume internal stability.

Pole placement changes A through feedback, rather than changing the transform used to inspect it. It can be derived directly in state space; Laplace and characteristic polynomials provide a compatible description. Frequency-domain feedback methods are also valid.
