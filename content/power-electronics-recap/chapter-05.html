5. Average and RMS: define these before calculating power

5.1 Definitions and the three useful numbers

For a real periodic waveform of period :

Average is signed. RMS is nonnegative and expresses an equivalent resistive-heating effect. The rectified average is another quantity; it must not be confused with the signed average of a bipolar signal.

Remember √2 for a sine, √D for a rectangular pulse, and √12 for peak-to-peak triangular ripple. They follow from integration, not from a universal conversion between peak and RMS.

Waveform, with stated amplitude Signed average RMS Peak/RMS crest factor
Constant 1
Sine with peak 0
Symmetric bipolar square, ±A 0 1
Unipolar rectangular pulse 0/A, duty D
Full-wave rectified sine, peak A
Half-wave rectified sine, peak A 2
Symmetric bipolar triangle, ±A 0
Zero-mean triangular ripple, peak-to-peak Δ 0

Square-wave distinction: a ±A square wave has RMS A. A 0/A square wave at 50% duty has average A/2 and RMS A/√2. Always draw the zero level and state the duty cycle.

For 10 A DC and 4 A peak-to-peak triangular ripple, the total RMS is approximately 10.066 A. The ripple RMS is only 1.155 A. In an ideal buck with an approximately constant load current, the output capacitor carries the ripple component, rather than the 10 A DC component.

If a known waveform segment occupies only a fraction D of the complete period, with zero elsewhere, its full-period average is multiplied by D and its RMS by √D, relative to the segment's own average and RMS. Mean squares add for orthogonal signals, including DC plus zero-mean ripple, different Fourier harmonics, and two currents that never flow simultaneously.
