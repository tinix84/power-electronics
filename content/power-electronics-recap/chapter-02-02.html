These connect geometry and material properties to inductance, transformer volt-seconds and eventual saturation. Relative permeability in a ferromagnetic material is generally not a fixed constant over all operating conditions.

Reluctance: the magnetic circuit's resistance. is the reluctance, in A/Wb (ampere-turns per weber). It plays the role of resistance in a magnetic circuit: the winding's ampere-turns drive flux through it, as voltage drives current through a resistor. Reluctances in series add, like resistors. Core data sheets give the inverse, the inductance factor in nH per turn², so that . Inductance grows with the square of the turns: double the turns, four times the inductance.

Predict. A ferrite core has a magnetic path length = 50 mm, cross-section = 50 mm² and = 2000. We cut a 0.5 mm air gap into the path. Which part sets the inductance: 50 mm of ferrite or 0.5 mm of air?

The gap is 1 % of the path, yet it holds 95 % of the total reluctance. falls from about 2510 nH to 120 nH, so 20 turns give 1.0 mH without the gap and 47.9 µH with it. The calculation ignores fringing, which makes the real gap look slightly larger in area.

Why accept 20 times less inductance? Suppose halves with temperature or DC bias. The ungapped inductor loses 50 % of its inductance; the gapped one loses only 4.5 %. The gap also stores most of the energy and raises the current at which the core saturates. Its cost is more turns for the same inductance, hence more copper loss, and fringing flux that heats nearby winding layers. Choose an ungapped core when inductance per turn matters most and the DC current is small, as in a common-mode choke. Choose a gapped core when the inductor carries DC, as in a buck. Powder cores spread the same gap through the material.
