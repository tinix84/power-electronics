19.7 Exercise: predict the change before solving the nonlinear circuit

Use the teaching models in Sections 19.2–19.4. This public practice exercise has a separate solution page.

Before calculating, predict the direction of each current change. State the variable that stays fixed when you take each slope.

A. A diode with a series resistor

Set the diode bias current to 2 mA through a 1 kΩ series resistor. Use the exponential model and fixed temperature from Section 19.2. Increase the supply voltage by 10 mV.

  1. Find the diode bias voltage and required supply voltage.
  2. Calculate the static and differential diode resistances.
  3. Draw the incremental source, series resistor and diode resistance.
  4. Predict the changes in diode current and diode voltage.
  5. Solve the nonlinear circuit after the supply change.
  6. Compare current changes and decide whether the tangent meets a 1% change-error limit.

B. Gate and drain changes in a MOSFET

Use the Section 19.3 operating point and square-law saturation model. Increase gate-source voltage by 50 mV and drain-source voltage by 0.5 V. Keep body bias and temperature fixed.

  1. Find the static drain ratio, , and .
  2. Predict the drain-current change from both perturbations.
  3. Evaluate the nonlinear drain-current change.
  4. Compare the changes against a 1% change-error limit.
  5. Explain what an output-resistor-only model misses.
  6. Choose a model or smaller perturbation range for the stated accuracy.

C. A solar panel with fixed illumination

Use the Section 19.4 model at 18 V. Keep irradiance and temperature fixed. Increase voltage by 0.2 V.

  1. Calculate delivered current, its derivative and the signed incremental ratio.
  2. Rewrite the incremental relation with passive current references.
  3. Predict the delivered-current change.
  4. Compare with the exact nonlinear change.
  5. Locate the maximum of delivered power.
  6. Check the derivative condition at that operating point.
  7. Explain why a light change needs an additional source term.

For each case, recommend a model for the specified perturbation. State one condition that would require a different model.
