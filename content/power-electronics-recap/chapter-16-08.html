16.8 Why does a larger gate resistor reduce coupling but increase heating? — Williams-inspired

Predict. Compare two MOSFET switching speeds at the same 48 V, 5 A and 100 kHz. Slower edges reduce parasitic displacement current, but do they reduce transistor loss?

Use a simple overlap-loss model: drain current is approximately 5 A during each linear drain-voltage transition. Compare equal turn-on and turn-off transition durations of 20 ns versus 100 ns:

Transition duration at each edge Cycle overlap energy Overlap loss at 100 kHz Current through 100 pF during voltage ramp
20 ns 4.8 µJ 0.48 W 0.24 A
100 ns 24 µJ 2.4 W 0.048 A

For an illustrative Miller charge Qgd = 5 nC, the corresponding approximately constant plateau currents are 0.25 A and 0.05 A, using t ≈ Qgd/Ig. If the driver has 5 V available across the total gate-path resistance during the plateau, the first estimates are 20 Ω and 100 Ω.

This is a local gate-charge estimate; Qgd, plateau voltage and driver impedance depend on operating conditions. Gate threshold voltage alone does not predict transition time.

Change one thing: vary the total gate resistance or separately control turn-on and turn-off currents. Slowing the edge reduces this specified capacitive current by five while increasing the model's overlap loss by five. It does not guarantee five times less radiated EMI: the full return paths, resonances and spectrum matter.

Conduction loss, output-capacitance energy, reverse recovery, dead time and detailed gate-loop dynamics are excluded, so the overlap numbers are not total MOSFET losses.

Why the basic matters: power is the area under v(t)i(t), while capacitive coupling depends on dv/dt. Williams's AN70 uses controlled slew rates to reduce switching-related interference; the numerical MOSFET example here is authored separately.

Fast or slow edge? The 20 ns case gives 0.48 W overlap loss and 0.24 A capacitive current. At 100 ns, these become 2.4 W and 0.048 A. We gain less coupling by accepting more transistor heat.

Keep 20 ns if that coupling meets the requirement. Move towards 100 ns only if the coupling reduction is needed and the added heat is acceptable. With no coupling limit specified, these calculations cannot select a final gate resistor.
