19.13 Exercise: select an LED driver and cooling path

A white XP-E2 LED must carry 350 mA from a fixed 5 V rail. Adjust each candidate at a junction temperature of 25 °C. Permitted current drift is ±5% over imposed junction temperatures of 25–125 °C.

The separate thermal design limit is 125 °C. The driver board and LED junction do not necessarily have the same temperature.

This is public worked practice with a dedicated solution page. Predict each result before running the supplied Python model.

A. Predict and compare the three drivers

Use the model and manufacturer anchors in Section 19.9.

  1. Find the LED voltage, static ratio and differential resistance at the initial operating point.
  2. Predict the current-change direction for each driver as temperature increases.
  3. Calculate the ballast resistance needed for the initial current.
  4. Solve each circuit at imposed junction temperatures of 25, 75 and 125 °C.
  5. Compare each current with the permitted range.
  6. Find the minimum ballast resistance that meets the upper current limit at 125 °C.
  7. Calculate the supply voltage needed to retain 350 mA with that resistor at 25 °C.
  8. Explain which requirement that alternative changes.

B. Add self-heating

Keep ambient temperature at 25 °C. Use the stated heat fraction and thermal capacitance. Compare board-to-air resistances of 20 and 140 K/W.

  1. Write the net heat-balance function for each driver.
  2. Find admissible thermal equilibria between 25 and 125 °C.
  3. Calculate the local thermal pole at each equilibrium.
  4. Predict whether a small temperature perturbation grows or decays.
  5. Run the nonlinear temperature trajectories from 25 °C.
  6. Stop at the design-temperature or current limit.
  7. Identify a case where current regulation passes while cooling fails.
  8. Explain why exceeding a limit does not prove an unstable equilibrium.

Then examine the exponential-only counter-model in Section 19.11.

  1. Locate its two equilibria.
  2. Classify both with the local pole.
  3. Compare trajectories starting 0.25 K above and below the unstable equilibrium.
  4. Explain why this counter-model cannot establish instability of the selected LED.

C. Screen the practical current-source concept

Use the circuit and component bounds in Section 19.12. Treat its component temperatures independently of the LED temperature.

  1. Find the setpoint for a 1 Ω sense resistor.
  2. Calculate the initial sense-resistor and MOSFET dissipation.
  3. Check load-path headroom and the amplifier supply requirement separately.
  4. Calculate the temperature-error interval after the 25 °C adjustment.
  5. Compare the interval with the ±5% requirement.
  6. List the checks still needed before this circuit becomes a selected hardware design.

D. Defend a choice

Recommend a driver and cooling condition for the original requirement. Support the recommendation with current error, loss and junction-temperature results.

State one changed requirement that could make the resistor alternative preferable. Explain which measurement would most reduce uncertainty in the thermal model.

The course supplies the numerical model. The private command is python scripts/led_electrothermal.py. The task is to predict, calculate and interpret its results, then defend the decision.
