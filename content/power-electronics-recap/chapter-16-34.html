16.34 The loop crosses 0 dB at −20 dB/decade: must it be stable?

Riddle: a negative-feedback loop has its first unity-gain crossover near 1 kHz with slope about −20 dB/decade. Its phase there is around −84°, apparently giving a generous positive phase margin. Is this enough to declare stability if a resonance appears later?

No. Test the complete loop, rather than one favourable local crossing. The following are authored continuous-time SISO loops, with all open-loop poles in the left half-plane and no imaginary-axis poles. They are a deliberately isolated control example, not fitted converter hardware. With unity negative feedback, characteristic roots solve 1 + L = 0.

A. A reference loop with one crossover

Define

The low-frequency pole is at 100 Hz; the second-order resonance is at 100 kHz. Its first and only gain crossover is 995.087 Hz, with slope −19.796 dB/decade, phase −84.273° and local phase margin +95.727°. The resonance peak remains around −6 dB, below unity. The full Nyquist curve has N = 0 with P = 0, and the three closed-loop poles all have negative real parts.

This loop is stable in the stated model.

Its phase reaches −180° at about 100.001 kHz, giving a gain-margin factor of about two, or +6.02 dB. That number is a gain-only margin of this stable nominal loop, not a substitute for checking a modified loop's new dynamics.

The corresponding −180° crossing is about 591.55 Hz, with gain-margin value −4.44 dB. Calling that a positive robustness margin would be incorrect: this nominal loop is already unstable.

C. The first crossing looks good, but the magnitude comes back

Keep A's form and parameters but reduce the resonant damping to ζC = 0.001. The first crossover remains almost identical to A, including its local slope and positive phase-margin quantity. The resonance now peaks around +14 dB, producing two more unity-gain crossings.

The first two locally positive angle differences do not overrule the final crossing or the full winding. Do not interpret a margin at an upward crossing using an unqualified single-downward-crossover rule. Case C has N = 2, P = 0 and two closed-loop RHP poles, approximately +2513.0 ± j628341.8 s⁻¹.

The loop is unstable even though the first crossing met the intended magnitude-slope target and appeared well separated from −180°.

Read the same cases in the complex plane

At each gain crossover, the Nyquist curve crosses the unit circle. At each −180° phase crossing, it crosses the negative real axis. The critical condition is their coincidence at −1, and the general stability result depends on the net winding of the complete contour and P.

Loop Number of unity crossings Open-loop RHP poles P Net clockwise turns N Closed-loop RHP poles Z = P + N Verdict
A: damped resonance 1 0 0 0 Stable
B: same magnitude, phase changed 1 0 2 2 Unstable
C: sharp resonance, recrossing 3 0 2 2 Unstable

Trace C's small resonant loop is traversed by both conjugate branches; overlapping geometric portions must be counted with their actual direction and multiplicity. A closed contour's winding is not just the number of visually distinct lobes. Positive-frequency data alone must not be counted as the complete contour.

The verdict is checked independently three ways: roots of the characteristic polynomial, eigenvalues of the explicit state-space negative-feedback connection, and numerical argument winding of 1 + L over the full contour. Crossing frequencies are also found both from a polynomial in ω² and by bracketed frequency-domain root searches. This avoids declaring stability from a sparse sweep that misses the narrow resonance.

Finally reconstruct each Nyquist sample from its Bode magnitude and phase using §12.4's equation. This demonstrates that complete Bode and Nyquist views contain the same frequency-response information; the one-crossing heuristic discards the context required by the general stability test.

Which loop can we accept? Keep case A as the stable reference. B has the same magnitude curve but fails through phase; C fails after the resonance creates more crossings. Neither is rescued by the first crossover's favourable appearance.

Lower bandwidth or added damping are redesign routes with response or implementation costs. After either change, repeat the full Nyquist and pole checks. Do not select a remedy from one local margin.
