19.6 Build and check a local model

  1. Define the terminal voltage and current directions.
  2. State the temperature, illumination and other terminal conditions that stay fixed.
  3. Solve the nonlinear bias circuit for its operating point.
  4. Calculate each required derivative at that point.
  5. Draw the circuit for perturbations, including controlled sources and relevant storage elements.
  6. Compare a finite perturbation with the nonlinear model.
  7. Decide whether the error changes the engineering conclusion.

Independent DC sources become zero only in the perturbation circuit. Their bias values still determine the operating point and derivative values. Keep dependent sources active.

Check the change you need to predict. A small error in total current can hide a large error in its incremental change. State whether the error limit concerns bias, gain, current change, distortion or settling.

For a smooth scalar curve, the next Taylor term is approximately . Compare it with the retained linear term over the intended range. Near a zero slope, relative slope-error measures can become misleading.

A differentiable curve can support a useful local model over a limited range. Discontinuities, hysteresis, region changes and internal dynamics can require more states or another representation. Small amplitude alone does not justify ignoring those effects.

Use the tangent model for local gain and impedance when its checked range fits the question. Use the nonlinear model for larger excursions or operating-region changes. A physical measurement can test both models near the actual bias point.

Course connection. The processed October 23 afternoon transcript, turns T009–T013, emphasizes small perturbations around an operating point. The examples here develop that local-model idea with explicit derivatives and error checks. Their numbers are authored additions.

Linear system models support superposition and transfer-function reasoning. Fourier and Laplace transforms also apply to many individual signals produced by nonlinear systems. Transforming a waveform does not make its generating system linear.
