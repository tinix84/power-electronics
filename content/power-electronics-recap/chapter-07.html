7. DC steady state, periodic steady state and transients

7.1 The DC impedance interpretation

For sinusoidal steady-state analysis:

As frequency approaches zero, an ideal inductor's impedance approaches zero and a capacitor's impedance grows without bound. This gives the familiar settled DC models: inductor short circuit and capacitor open circuit, provided a bounded DC equilibrium actually exists.

The inductor can carry nonzero DC current; the capacitor can retain nonzero DC voltage. A constant voltage across an ideal inductor instead makes its current ramp forever, so that circuit has no bounded DC steady state. Similarly, constant nonzero capacitor current ramps its voltage.
