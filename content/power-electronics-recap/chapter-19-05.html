19.5 A controlled constant-power load: negative slope while absorbing power

Return to passive references: current enters the positive-voltage terminal. An ideal constant-input-power load obeys . With fixed:

At 60 W and 12 V, current is 5 A. The static ratio is +2.4 Ω, while incremental resistance is −2.4 Ω. A −0.1 V voltage perturbation predicts a +41.667 mA current change. The exact constant-power relation gives +42.017 mA.

The load still absorbs positive power. Its controller demands more current when the voltage decreases. The negative incremental resistance describes that response.

This is different from the solar-panel sign change above. The constant-power load has a negative slope even with passive current references. Its input power does not reverse.

The approximation requires the controller to maintain input power over the perturbation's time scale. Current limits, loss variation and control bandwidth change the response. A real converter generally has a frequency-dependent input impedance.

Section 12.5 connects this response to source impedance and input-filter interaction. A slope alone does not establish closed-loop stability.
