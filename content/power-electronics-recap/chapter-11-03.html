11.3 Parallel resonator: an impedance maximum

For an ideal L and C in parallel with an explicit shunt loss :

The parallel circuit is shown alongside the series network in section 11.2; all three branches connect across the same port terminals a and b.

At resonance the susceptances cancel and the input impedance is . Below resonance it is inductive; above resonance it is capacitive. Large inductor and capacitor currents can circulate internally despite relatively small input current.

With L = 100 µH and C = 1 µF, resonance is 15.915 kHz. Choose Ω for the series example and kΩ for the parallel example; the respective quality factors are 10 and 100. These are distinct loss models, rather than equivalent descriptions of one physical lossy component.

Why this matters: a capacitor with ESL behaves as a series resonator and becomes inductive above self-resonance. Several decoupling branches can create parallel antiresonance peaks. These peaks can violate a PDN target impedance. An EMI filter's attenuation also depends on source/load impedances and damping, not only its nominal L and C.
