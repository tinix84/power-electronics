18.2 Reference voltages: loading, tolerance and acquisition time

A resistive divider sets a fraction of its source voltage. It does not create an independent precision reference. Supply variation still appears at its output.

Compare two dividers from 12 V to a nominal 3 V. Both have a 3:1 upper-to-lower resistance ratio. The measurement input has a constant 1 MΩ resistance.

Upper / lower resistor Divider current without load Power without load Source resistance Loaded output
30 kΩ / 10 kΩ 0.300 mA 3.60 mW 7.50 kΩ 2.9777 V
3 kΩ / 1 kΩ 3.000 mA 36.0 mW 0.750 kΩ 2.9978 V

The larger resistors reduce standing power by ten. They increase the loading error from about 0.075% to 0.744%. A buffer can reduce loading, but its offset, bias current and settling also need checks.

Resistor tolerance affects the ratio. With independent ±0.1% limits on the 30 kΩ and 10 kΩ resistors, the unloaded output spans approximately 2.9955–3.0045 V. A ratio-matched network may control this error better than two unrelated resistor tolerances.

An analog-to-digital converter also loads the divider dynamically. Its acquisition input can include a switched capacitor. A DC input-resistance calculation does not establish settling accuracy.
