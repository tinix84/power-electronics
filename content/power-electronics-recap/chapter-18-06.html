18.6 Calorimetry: read temperature, then infer heat or power

A resistive thermometer can occupy a bridge arm and sense a calorimeter's temperature rise. The bridge provides an electrical readout. A thermal model and calibration connect that readout to heat or power.

At 2 s after the power step, the rise is only about 0.632 K. A bridge reading before thermal settling does not equal the steady-state response.

For an energy pulse, heat capacity also matters. The short-pulse estimate neglects heat lost during the pulse. Use the dynamic model when that loss affects the required accuracy.

The readout also heats the sensor. At bridge balance, the 1 kΩ sensing arm dissipates 0.25 mW with 1 V excitation. If all that heat enters the modeled thermal body, it adds 0.025 K. Doubling excitation quadruples this heating.

The thermal coupling of the real sensor and its leads determines the actual disturbance. Include that coupling and the baseline heat input in the calibration.
