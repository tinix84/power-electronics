19.12 A practical linear current-source concept

A current source needs a reference, current measurement and an output device. Use an op-amp to compare a 0.350 V setpoint with the voltage across a 1 Ω sense resistor.

Adjust the nominal divider ratio at 25 °C to obtain 350 mA. The adjustment removes initial error at that condition. It does not remove subsequent temperature drift.

Check voltage and power before accuracy

At the initial LED voltage of 2.930 V, the sense resistor drops 0.350 V. The MOSFET has 1.720 V across drain and source. The teaching compliance allowance is 0.300 V.

The OPA197 requires at least 4.5 V supply. Its own supply requirement still applies even when the LED load path has enough headroom. Reference and amplifier quiescent losses are additional to this load-path balance.

The transistor is a circuit requirement, not a completed device selection. Its DC safe operating area, available gate drive and cooling require checks. The 0.300 V allowance is an authored model parameter.

A bounded current-error budget

Keep driver-component temperatures independent of LED junction temperature. Each component may range from 25 to 125 °C in this conservative comparison. The calculation enumerates endpoint error signs after the initial adjustment.

Contribution Selected basis
Reference REF5025-EP: 0.05% initial accuracy and 6.5 ppm/K maximum box drift.
Divider resistors TNPW e3: selected 0.1% tolerance and 25 ppm/K grade.
Complete 1 Ω shunt WSR2: selected 0.5% tolerance and 75 ppm/K component TCR.
Amplifier OPA197: 100 µV offset and 2.5 µV/K drift bounds in the applicable input stage.
Additional amplifier terms Input bias, common-mode transfer and supply-rejection allowances.

The reference specification uses a box method over −55 to 125 °C. Use its complete 180 K span as a bound on peak-to-peak drift. Do not interpret that specification as a guaranteed local derivative at every temperature.

The 256 enumerated corners give approximately 345.10–354.98 mA, or −1.40% to +1.42%. Those declared DC contributions fit inside the ±5% current requirement. They do not establish a complete hardware accuracy guarantee.

Use the complete shunt's 75 ppm/K specification. Its less-than-20 ppm/K resistive-element value does not include the complete component behavior.

The feedback model is quasi-static on the thermal time scale. It does not prove electronic loop stability with a particular MOSFET gate capacitance. Layout, compensation, reference hysteresis, aging and device thermal qualification remain outside this calculation.

The component bounds come from REF5025-EP, OPA197, WSR and TNPW e3 manufacturer datasheets. The private reference metadata records the revisions and test conditions.
