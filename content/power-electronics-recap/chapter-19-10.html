19.10 Three drivers, one initial current

Use the same LED model and 5 V rail in all three cases. Adjust every driver to 350 mA at a junction temperature of 25 °C. Keep temperature imposed during this first comparison.

A. A voltage regulator holds voltage

Set the regulator output to 2.930 V. Its output stays fixed while the LED curve moves with temperature. The model therefore demands more current as the junction warms.

At 125 °C, current reaches about 691.55 mA, an increase of 97.59%. A regulated voltage is not a regulated LED current.

This result assumes that the voltage regulator can supply the demanded current. Current limiting or loss of voltage regulation changes the circuit. The exercise does not infer physical damage from the calculated current.

C. A current regulator adjusts voltage

The ideal current regulator holds 350 mA while the model's required LED voltage falls from 2.930 V to 2.780 V. Its current error is zero only in this ideal comparison.

A real current regulator has limited headroom, reference drift and measurement errors. Section 19.12 evaluates those limits.

That resistor solution changes the specified 5 V rail. It is a credible alternative when a higher rail and its additional loss are acceptable.

At the initial operating point, every arrangement draws 1.750 W through its load path. The LED receives 1.0255 W. The remaining 0.7245 W becomes driver or resistor loss. Current regulation improves control of current, not the ideal linear-stage conversion ratio. Driver quiescent power is additional.
