Circuit foundations

Circuit and electronics foundations for power electronics

Audience: electrical engineering students refreshing earlier circuit courses.
Teaching time: two sessions of 90 minutes.
Purpose: connect circuit laws, waveforms and system models to the design of real power converters.

Use this lesson in two ways. Follow the teaching route for a refresher on circuit foundations. Later, return with a symptom and use chapter 16 to find a worked example.

Most numerical examples are teaching scenarios. Device data and published measurements are identified where used. Voltages and currents follow the passive sign convention unless stated otherwise.

The aim is to learn how an engineer approaches an unfamiliar circuit. Start with what you can observe. Make a prediction, choose a model and check the result. The equation should help you make a decision. Explain the benefit and cost of your choice. When another route is credible, say what would make you choose it.

Course organisation and assessment

The following arrangements were announced in the introductory lesson on 16 October 2026. They apply to the course as a whole, separately from the technical recap and its suggested teaching route below.

  • Project and examination: choose a power-electronics kit from the course list, preferably a different kit for each student. Analyse its operation using the project checklist, calculations and simulation. Bring the project to the examination and explain its strengths, limitations and possible improvements, including what happens when a component value changes. The exercise mirrors an engineering assignment: understand an existing design well enough to evaluate and improve it. Project material was announced as available on the lecturer's GitHub; use the course-provided repository and checklist.
  • Mandatory exercises: assigned exercises contribute to the evaluation and must be submitted by the next lesson. Final numerical results will be supplied for comparison; these are not complete worked derivations. Regular work throughout the course is part of the assessment. No numerical weighting between exercises and the project examination was specified in the recording.
  • Books, AI and other tools: these may be used for the project and exercises. Students must understand the submitted reasoning and be able to explain and defend their calculations, models and design decisions themselves.
  • Interactive lessons: interrupt when an explanation is unclear, question assumptions and point out errors. The aim is to practise engineering judgement and problem solving, with regular breaks during the teaching afternoon.
  • Proposed company visit: a visit near the end of the course in January was discussed, potentially to Regatron in Arbon to see power supplies and EMC facilities. The destination and arrangements were tentative, not a confirmed booking.

Engineering approach: estimate, model, then verify

Before opening a simulator, sketch the current path. Mark the voltage references and stored energy. Estimate the expected voltage, current or time scale. Check the units.

Choose the simplest model that can answer your question. Compare its result with your estimate. If they disagree, inspect the assumptions before adding more detail. Add a parasitic or nonlinearity when it could change the decision.

A solver can print six digits without knowing the actual capacitance under bias or the winding temperature. More digits do not make uncertain inputs accurate. Use component data, vary uncertain values and compare with measurements where available.

The roughly 30% uncertainty discussed in the lesson was an example, not a general accuracy limit. Ask what the model can establish. A simple model may explain the failure even when it cannot reproduce every switching edge.

Learning outcomes and teaching route

By the end, you should be able to:

  • Draw the source, load and complete current path.
  • Use circuit laws and equivalent sources to explain loading.
  • Sketch R/L/C waveforms and apply transformer winding conventions.
  • Choose mean, RMS or instantaneous values for the calculation.
  • Estimate parasitic effects and check periodic balance.
  • Read impedance and Bode plots within the model's limits.
  • Choose a regulation architecture and explain the trade-off.
