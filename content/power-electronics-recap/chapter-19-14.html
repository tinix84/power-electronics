All poles are negative. The voltage regulator's increased current settles, but remains outside the allowed current band. This is a stable electrical-accuracy failure, not thermal runaway.

With the 140 K/W board path, no equilibrium satisfies the 125 °C design limit. The trajectories stop at 125 °C:

The current regulator continues to meet its ideal current target while the thermal requirement fails. Better current regulation cannot replace the missing cooling path.

C. A genuine unstable equilibrium in the explicit counter-model

Removing fitted series resistance changes the model. With the counter-model's 9.10125 K/W total thermal resistance, the equilibria are 39.333 and 51.994 °C.

The corresponding poles are −0.06227 and +0.07685 s⁻¹. Small temperature errors decay at the lower root and grow at the upper root. The nonlinear integrations confirm those signs.

At the upper root, current is 1.34972 A. A positive perturbation drives current upward until the declared current limit stops the simulation. A negative perturbation moves toward the lower equilibrium.

The anchored model under the same cooling gives a different heat-balance curve. The exponential-only result is a model-sensitivity demonstration, not an XP-E2 instability claim.

The reference box-drift allowance uses its full specified temperature span. The shunt uses complete-component TCR. Input-bias and common-mode transfer allowances supplement the offset and drift terms.

E. Conditional recommendation

For the original 5 V rail and full imposed junction-temperature range, choose current regulation with a cooling path that meets the separate temperature requirement. The modeled 20 K/W board path meets that thermal requirement at 25 °C ambient.

The component-based DC screen supports the current-regulation concept. It is not a completed hardware qualification. The 140 K/W path fails despite accurate current.

For a narrower temperature range, a relaxed current tolerance or a higher permitted rail, reconsider the resistor solution. It has fewer active components but retains temperature drift and continuous loss.

Measure the actual junction-to-ambient path or a calibrated temperature transient to reduce the thermal uncertainty. Report any assumption about emitted optical power when converting electrical power into junction heat.
