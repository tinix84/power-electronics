The ratio predicts loading better than either resistance alone. A load resistance described as high or low needs this reference.

2. Lower source resistance costs standing power

With the load disconnected, supply current is . Both resistors dissipate power continuously:

Divider B reduces the source resistance by ten and increases standing loss by ten. It consumes 720 mW while supplying no load power.

Standing loss is the unloaded baseline. Loading changes the resistor voltages and their actual dissipation. Do not treat the unloaded loss as the total loss at every load.

Supply accuracy, resistor ratio, temperature drift, measurement bias and acquisition settling require additional error allowances. A 1% total accuracy requirement leaves less than 1% for loading.

4. The ideal op-amp follower separates the current paths

The ideal input draws no current, so divider A remains unloaded. The output is 6 V. The 100 Ω load draws 60 mA and absorbs 360 mW.

For the stated linear output-stage model, the active stage draws 60 mA from 12 V. Its dissipation is:

The divider still consumes 72 mW. Total supply current is 66 mA, giving 792 mW input power. The balance is 360 mW load power, 360 mW active-stage loss and 72 mW divider loss.

The buffer improves load regulation without the 720 mW standing divider loss of circuit B. It does not eliminate conversion loss. In this idealized model, active-stage load loss falls to zero when the load disconnects. A real amplifier also consumes quiescent power.

The calculated 60 mA exceeds a hypothetical 40 mA output limit. That amplifier cannot maintain the assumed 6 V across 100 Ω. Its actual voltage requires its limiting and thermal models.

Low input current and low output impedance provide practical decoupling within specified operating limits. A buffer does not make the supply-derived reference immune to supply variation. Chapter 14 compares this linear power loss with other supply architectures.

Most load current comes through the collector. The divider supplies only the base current, which still reduces its output by 0.250 V. The assumed base-emitter drop removes another 0.700 V.

The model gives an effective resistance term in the emitter-current equation. It omits the transistor's incremental junction resistance and other nonlinear effects. It is not a complete small-signal output-impedance model.

The transistor improves current drive but does not maintain the op-amp model's 6 V output. Feedback around a suitable transistor stage can correct voltage error. It also introduces further stability and operating-range requirements.

6. Design conclusion

Specify reference accuracy, load range and permitted dissipation together. A low-resistance divider trades standing power for lower source resistance. A buffer separates reference sensing from load-current delivery within its operating limits.
