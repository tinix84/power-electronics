16.12 Optional extension: select real snubber components and include the PCB

Question: can the 8.2 Ω / 1.2 nF design be built with ordinary surface-mount parts, and will it behave the same when placed several connections away from the device? Retain section 16.11's 48 V, 5 ns edge, 100 kHz fixture. Separate a datasheet screening calculation from final hardware qualification.

A. A concrete pair of component candidates

Function Exact candidate Selected value
Pulse-proof resistor Vishay CRCW12068R20FKEAHP 8.2 Ω ±1%, 1206, HP e3
Stable snubber capacitor KEMET C0805C122J1GACTU 1.2 nF ±5%, 100 V, C0G, 0805

The resistor's P70 rating is 0.75 W, conditional on the stated mounting/thermal resistance, with ambient derating above 70°C and a 155°C film-temperature limit. Its continuous-pulse and pulse-voltage charts start at 1 µs. Use these rather than its single-pulse curve for repetitive switching. See Vishay document 20043, revision 17 March 2026, pp. 1, 3, 5–6.

The capacitor code identifies 122 = 1200 pF, J = ±5%, 1 = 100 V, G = C0G. The family specifies −55°C to +125°C operation and stable capacitance with applied DC voltage. Its short-duration dielectric-withstand test is not an operating-voltage allowance. See KEMET C1003, 20 February 2025, pp. 1 and 4–5.

These are real ordering codes; the simulations below use their nominal R/C values and initial tolerances. Neither manufacturer supplies the complete nanosecond package/PCB model used here. Saved manufacturer PDFs and SHA-256 hashes are recorded with the numerical results.

The nominal mean uses 51% of that allowance. At 125°C, the same calculation permits only 0.2647 W, so this candidate fails the nominal mean-power check there. The power rating requires adequate PCB heat removal; package size alone does not establish it. The resistor's headline voltage limit also does not permit arbitrary continuous voltage across 8.2 Ω: continuous power remains a separate constraint.

Repetitive-pulse screening: reading the 1206-HP curve at its 1 µs left endpoint, choose 400 W as an intentionally conservative screening reference below the plotted curve, not a precisely digitized rating. Both the 167 W simulated peak and the separate 281 W discharge estimate lie below it.

At a +10% bus, the latter becomes approximately 340 W before resistance tolerance. Nominal resistor peak voltage is only 37.0 V, or 48 V in the separate discharge estimate, well below the pulse-voltage curve at 1 µs.

What this establishes: reasonable average-power and coarse peak-stress margins for a candidate. What remains open: the published curve does not qualify 9 ns equivalent pulses. Do not extrapolate its slope into the nanosecond region or label this a manufacturer-guaranteed repetitive-pulse pass. Obtain manufacturer confirmation for the actual waveform and verify heating, resistance drift and failures on hardware.

A 1 µs rectangle at the actual repetition rate would have a different, much larger mean power; it is not the loss calculation used here.

C. Capacitor voltage is not the switching-node voltage

The resistor separates the two nodes. With zero added branch inductance, the switching node reaches 63.73 V, while the actual snubber capacitor reaches only 48.01 V. Use the voltage across the capacitor's own terminals for its rating check. For bipolar or offset waveforms check the largest absolute terminal voltage, including ringing; do not compare only the AC RMS component with a DC rating.

The following sensitivity sweep includes R ±1%, C ±5%, a +10% bus and all five branch-inductance cases below. Each maximum is taken independently; they need not occur in the same corner.

Initial component tolerance is included; temperature drift, aging, edge-speed variation and uncertain extracted tank parameters are not. These are bounded calculations for the stated fixture, not a universal worst case.

The capacitor must additionally carry the computed current. The family datasheet does not give a part-specific repetitive-current or MHz ESR/thermal limit sufficient to complete that qualification. A low-frequency dissipation-factor measurement cannot simply be turned into an ESR valid across the nanosecond pulse spectrum. Use a suitable high-frequency component model and thermal verification; a voltage pass alone does not close the current/heating check.

Keep the original commutation inductance Lp = 25.33 nH. Add Lbranch in series with Rs and Cs, representing the combined package ESL, pads, outgoing trace and return. The values 0, 1, 5, 20 and 100 nH are assumed sensitivity cases, not manufacturer specifications or lengths converted with a universal nH/mm rule.

Do not hide the non-monotonic result. A modest inductance changes the tuning and can reduce the first node peak in this fixture. At 20 nH the capacitor stress and settling time already increase. At 100 nH the branch supplies little damping at the original 50 MHz ring: its reactance there is approximately 28.8 Ω, compared with −2.65 Ω for the ideal RC branch.

Overshoot and ringing duration then increase, even though mean resistor heating decreases slightly. Less resistor heating can mean that less unwanted energy reached the intended damper.

The series-LC reactance crosses zero at approximately 145, 65.0, 32.5 and 14.5 MHz for the four nonzero cases. Because the branch includes 8.2 Ω, its impedance minimum is R, not zero. The full network has interacting modes; this branch frequency alone does not predict the waveform.

E. Translate the model into PCB placement

Place the series RC branch across the same two physical terminals whose ringing is being damped. Keep the outgoing connection and return close together and minimize the enclosed loop. A nearby capacitor with a long connection to a distant ground via can still form a poor snubber. In an actual switch-node-to-return design, check the local power return rather than assuming every ground symbol is equipotential at 50 MHz.

Give the resistor copper for heat removal while keeping its high-frequency loop compact. Keep the branch away from sensitive signal loops; coupling is absent from this model. If two layers or parallel paths are used, include their actual return geometry and vias in the inductance extraction. Smaller packages may reduce ESL but can also reduce thermal/pulse capability: geometry and ratings must be considered together.

Measure with a short probe connection or a suitable differential/coaxial fixture, then repeat the ringing extraction and tuning on the actual layout. This linear example has no nonlinear Coss, reverse recovery, diode clamps or mutual coupling. The simulated 71.7 V peak is not a switch-voltage rating approval.

Component decision. Retain the named resistor and capacitor as candidates, not as a completed selection. The available resistor chart does not qualify the nanosecond repetitive pulse. Keep the compact loop and obtain suitable pulse evidence before accepting the pair.

A different part or footprint is an alternative if qualification fails. It may improve pulse capability, but changes layout and branch inductance. Repeat the waveform check after that change.
