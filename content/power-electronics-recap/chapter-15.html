Circuit foundations

15. Exit questions with short answers

  1. Why does a loaded divider fall below its nominal voltage? The load draws current through the source's Thévenin resistance.

  2. What is the RMS of a ±10 A square wave? Of 0/10 A at 25% duty? 10 A and 5 A; their signed means are 0 A and 2.5 A.

  3. Can current with zero mean heat a wire? Yes. Heating depends on the mean square.

  4. Does reactive power disappear as heat in an ideal capacitor? No; energy is exchanged. The resulting RMS current can increase real losses elsewhere.

  5. Does zero average inductor voltage imply zero instantaneous voltage? No. Switching intervals can have opposite nonzero voltages.

  6. Why does the CPU example need local energy storage? The remote interconnect cannot change its current fast enough within the voltage budget.

  7. Is the crosstalk voltage transfer low-pass or high-pass in the defined victim model? High-pass. The injected-current-to-voltage impedance is low-pass.

  8. Can a nonlinear system's output be Fourier analysed? Yes; that does not make the system describable by one linear transfer function.

  9. Does the switching harmonic define a second RC time constant? No. The circuit pole sets settling; switching supplies forced frequencies.

  10. Which resonator produces an impedance peak? The defined parallel RLC; the series RLC produces a dip.

  11. Why use an LC output filter in Class-D? Stronger high-frequency attenuation with energy storage instead of a large dissipative series resistor.

  12. When is an LDO efficient? When the output/input voltage ratio is close to one and its own current is small relative to the load, while adequate dropout headroom remains.

  13. Why can a 60 W load have −2.4 Ω incremental resistance while absorbing power? At 12 V it draws 5 A, and its regulation increases current when voltage falls; the DC voltage/current ratio remains positive.

  14. Does an input-filter impedance crossing prove instability? No. It fails a conservative magnitude check; the interaction-loop phase, poles and Nyquist behaviour determine the general result.

  15. What changes when the same overlap's dielectric spacing halves? Plate-model capacitance and current into a stationary reference double. The moving victim's voltage response must include the changed time constant and divider ratio.

  16. What does a 4:1 ideal transformer do to a 6 Ω secondary load? It presents 96 Ω at the primary; voltage scales by 4 and current magnitudes inversely.

  17. Why is an ideal turns-ratio element insufficient for flyback analysis? The magnetizing inductance stores energy and its flux must be tracked and reset.

  18. What changes when one winding of a series-coupled pair is reversed? The mutual contribution changes from +2M to −2M in the series equivalent inductance.

  19. For L₁ = 100 µH, L₂ = 25 µH and k = 0.8, what are M and the short-circuit primary inductance? M = 40 µH and L₁,short = 36 µH.

  20. Why can a real transformer draw current with its secondary open? Core excitation requires magnetizing current and core loss requires active current; both pass through the primary winding.

  21. Why does the 48 V, 4:1 real example deliver 11.704 V rather than 12 V? Primary and secondary winding impedances cause load-dependent drops; its internal induced-voltage ratio still follows the turns ratio.

  22. What happens to copper skin depth when frequency increases by a factor of four? It halves, with resistivity and permeability held fixed.

  23. Should a DC-plus-ripple current use the switching-frequency resistance for all its RMS current? No. Use RDC for the DC component and frequency-dependent AC resistance for each harmonic; include proximity effects where relevant.

  24. Does one radial layer remove all high-frequency copper loss? No. The worked round-wire case already has 93.01 W of skin-only loss; adjacent conductors can add proximity loss.

  25. In the specified four-layer-per-winding comparison, why does interleaving reduce proximity loss? It reduces the sum of squared layer mean fields from 42K² to 2K² while keeping the turns ratio and load waveform unchanged.

  26. Does zero source impedance at the IC attenuate supply-voltage noise? No; that noise-transfer ratio is one, while load-current-induced voltage ripple is zero in the same ideal model.

  27. Does a 4.47 cm PCB route necessarily block a 1 GHz signal? No. The defined matched, lossless line gives unit amplitude and approximately 0.298 ns propagation delay.

  28. What target-impedance scale follows from a 100 A change and 30 mV allowance? 0.30 mΩ at the defined port, over the relevant band; the teaching numbers are not a processor specification.

  29. Why can a 10 Ω RC supply filter be attractive at 10 mA but fail at 100 mA? It drops 100 mV and dissipates 1 mW at 10 mA; at 100 mA it drops 1 V even though its 100 mW loss is below the illustrative 125 mW allowance.

  30. Does a ferrite bead's AC loss guarantee a nonresonant supply filter? No. Its loss may become significant above the resonance of its low-frequency inductance and the local capacitor; inspect the complete complex impedance and load.

  31. Why can a classic 78xx-type regulator not make 3.3 V from 5 V? Its NPN-Darlington pass structure needs about 2.5 V of headroom and only 1.7 V is available; an LDO with a dropout below 0.5 V can, at a maximum efficiency of 66%.

  32. Which two numbers turn the emitter-follower/Zener regulator into a Thévenin source? The line ratio sets how the open-circuit voltage follows the input, and is its series resistance.

  33. When does a linear regulator beat a switching converter on efficiency? When exceeds the switcher's efficiency at that load: 3.6 V to 3.3 V gives 91.7% from a linear stage, with no switching ripple to filter.
