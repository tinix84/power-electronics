With 5 V excitation and a 1% resistance increase, the exact signal is 12.438 mV. The linear estimate is 12.500 mV. Its relative error is 0.5%. At a 10% resistance change, that approximation error reaches 5%.

The sensor can respond to strain, pressure or temperature. The complete transducer needs the relation between that physical quantity and its resistance changes.

Ratiometric measurement. If the converter reference follows the bridge excitation, its result can depend on . This reduces sensitivity to common excitation changes within the measurement bandwidth. Analog Devices CN0601 shows this arrangement.

Higher excitation increases the output signal. It also increases resistor heating. Ratio measurement does not cancel temperature changes caused by that heating.

Lead resistance, amplifier bias current, common-mode range and resistor tracking still matter. Choose excitation and gain from the signal requirement and the permitted sensor disturbance.
