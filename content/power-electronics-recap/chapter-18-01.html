Do not replace these complex impedances with their magnitudes before adding them. Phase cancellation near resonance can change both gain and component stress.

Two equal ratios can give different voltage sources. Compare two dividers connected to the same ideal 12 V supply:

A 1 kΩ / 1 kΩ 6 V 500 Ω 72 mW

With no load, a voltage measurement suggests that they are equivalent. Their source resistances reveal the difference. For either divider:

A load much larger than the source resistance causes little voltage reduction. An equal load halves the unloaded voltage. A smaller load reduces it further. For divider A, the original 1 kΩ load still gives 4 V.

Divider B approaches an ideal voltage source more closely over the same load range. Its unloaded loss is ten times higher. That loss continues even when the load is disconnected.

A reference divider therefore needs a defined load and an error limit. A buffer reduces reference loading and supplies load current through an active output stage. It still needs supply power and has operating limits.
