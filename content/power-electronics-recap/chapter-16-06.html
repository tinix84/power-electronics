16.6 Where does leakage energy go when the switch opens? — Basso-inspired

Predict. An ideal-transformer model works, but the real switch exhibits a sharp drain spike. Add the leakage energy and a receiving capacitance before choosing a voltage rating or clamp.

Use an isolated commutation cell, with Lleak = 1 µH, initial current magnitude 2 A, and an initially empty 100 pF capacitance. These are incremental cell variables, not the complete flyback drain voltage. Initially stored energy is 2 µJ. Without damping or a clamp, conservation gives the first voltage-amplitude scale:

Its natural frequency is 15.92 MHz. A parallel 50 Ω resistor gives critical damping for this particular parallel RLC cell, since Rcrit = ½√(L/C). This is an equivalent damping illustration, not a ready-to-use series-RC snubber value or a resistor continuously connected across a real drain supply.

Alternatively, an ideal 100 V clamp begins conducting at 5.236 ns, when the remaining inductor current is 1.732 A. Holding the cell at 100 V reduces that current to zero in 17.32 ns, so the first clamp interval ends at 22.56 ns. The clamp absorbs 1.5 µJ; 0.5 µJ remains on the 100 pF capacitor.

Their sum is the original 2 µJ. The ideal clamp voltage source represents an energy sink, not a complete TVS/RCD implementation.

Dissipating the entire 2 µJ every cycle at 100 kHz means 0.2 W. The plotted first-clamp-event contribution alone is 0.15 W at that repetition rate, but the remaining capacitor energy also needs an eventual destination. A real clamp may absorb additional energy from the rest of the converter.

Add input voltage, reflected output voltage, nonlinear device capacitance and the actual topology before calculating absolute MOSFET VDS or total clamp power.

Change one thing: vary leakage, turn-off current or receiving capacitance; observe the square-law energy dependence on current. This exercise connects the earlier coupled-inductor model to switching commutation. See Basso's Trade Secrets of the Flyback Converter, particularly its leakage-inductance discussion. The plotted cell is an authored simplification.

Clamp or damping? Use the ideal 100 V clamp to study a bounded first excursion. It absorbs 1.5 µJ, while 0.5 µJ remains on the capacitor. The 50 Ω alternative damps the ring instead of imposing a clamp voltage.

Keep the clamp as this voltage-limiting reference, and account for the remaining energy. A real clamp circuit needs its own voltage and repetitive-power checks. The parallel resistor is not a drop-in drain snubber.
