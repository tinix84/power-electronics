A negative pole predicts local decay. A positive pole predicts local growth. A zero pole requires further analysis. The derivative at a point that is not an equilibrium does not establish equilibrium stability.

With the 20 K/W board path, all three cases have stable equilibria inside the design range:

Driver Equilibrium junction temperature LED current
Voltage regulator 49.386 °C 430.122 mA
Series resistor 44.919 °C 354.684 mA
Current regulator 44.644 °C 350.000 mA

The voltage-driven case is thermally stable here, despite its substantial current drift. Stable does not mean compliant with the current requirement.

With the 140 K/W board path, no admissible equilibrium appears below 125 °C. The simulations stop at that limit after approximately 62.87 s, 154.40 s and 197.44 s, respectively.

The current-regulated case still carries exactly 350 mA when it reaches the temperature limit. Its current control passes while its cooling fails. These results establish a design-limit violation, not a particular physical failure mode.

A counter-model exposes an approximation error

The anchored model does not produce an unstable equilibrium inside this design domain. Do not alter its source data to force one.

For a separate mathematical comparison, remove the fitted series resistance and use . Keep the same initial voltage, current, logarithmic scale and heat fraction. This exponential-only circuit is not an XP-E2 prediction.

It has two equilibria in the teaching model:

Equilibrium Temperature Current Thermal pole
Stable 39.333 °C 716.639 mA −0.06227 s⁻¹
Unstable 51.994 °C 1.34972 A +0.07685 s⁻¹

Small perturbations about the second equilibrium move away from it. The rising trajectory stops at the declared current limit. Restoring the fitted series resistance changes the conclusion under the same cooling.

This is why model simplification needs an error check. An unstable equilibrium in a simplified circuit does not prove instability of the selected LED.

Cooling sensitivity

Changing the assumed heat fraction changes the required cooling. The plotted boundary uses 350 mA and the 125 °C design temperature. It is a model-based design screen, not a measured package or heatsink rating.
