10. What if? Four variations of the finished design

The base design runs in continuous conduction at full load over the whole range. Appendix D.1 gives the condition: with K=2Lfs/RK = 2Lf_s/RK=2Lfs​/R, CCM holds while K>Kcrit=D(1−D)2K > K_{\mathrm{crit}} = D(1-D)^2K>Kcrit​=D(1−D)2. That boundary has its maximum, 4/27 = 0.148, at DDD = 1/3 (Appendix D.1). At full load KKK = 0.213 (ours), above the maximum, so full load is CCM at every input. 10.1 Critical (boundary) conduction: No!

Hold the current triangle so that it just touches zero each cycle. Its ripple is then twice the input current, and the frequency or the inductance follows:

Ipk=2 Iin,fs=Vin D2 L Iin,L=Vin D2 fs Iin(10.1a)I_{\mathrm{pk}}=2\,I_{\mathrm{in}},\qquad f_s=\frac{V_{\mathrm{in}}\,D}{2\,L\,I_{\mathrm{in}}},\qquad L=\frac{V_{\mathrm{in}}\,D}{2\,f_s\,I_{\mathrm{in}}}\tag{10.1a}Ipk​=2Iin​,fs​=2LIin​Vin​D​,L=2fs​Iin​Vin​D​(10.1a)

Ipk=22.5 A,fs=12⋅0.7782⋅10 μH⋅11.25=41.5 kHz,L=12⋅0.7782⋅230 kHz⋅11.25=1.80 μH(10.1b)I_{\mathrm{pk}}=22.5\ \mathrm{A},\qquad f_s=\frac{12\cdot0.778}{2\cdot10\ \mu\mathrm{H}\cdot11.25}=41.5\ \mathrm{kHz},\qquad L=\frac{12\cdot0.778}{2\cdot230\ \mathrm{kHz}\cdot11.25}=1.80\ \mu\mathrm{H}\tag{10.1b}Ipk​=22.5 A,fs​=2⋅10 μH⋅11.2512⋅0.778​=41.5 kHz,L=2⋅230 kHz⋅11.2512⋅0.778​=1.80 μH(10.1b)

Keeping TI’s 10 µH means running at 41.5 kHz (ours). Keeping 230 kHz needs a 1.80 µH inductor rated for 22.5 A. Either way the peak is 22.5 A at 12 V. That is far above the 18.0 A at which TI’s ferrite core has lost 10 %, and above the 21.5 A of the 20 % drop (TI’s part). No! A hard-saturating core cannot run there. The RMS current rises to 2Iin/32I_{\mathrm{in}}/\sqrt{3}2Iin​/3​ = 13.0 A (ours).

What critical conduction buys. The low side turns on at zero current. The turn-on overlap, 1.51 W at 12 V, and the reverse recovery, 0.46 W, disappear (§8.1). Those are the two largest switching terms of the boost. The output charge is still lost unless the current goes slightly negative (§6.2). The frequency then moves with load and input: at half the load, twice the frequency.

When would we choose the boundary? Not on this board. Its inductor, its 18.75 A current limit and its fixed-frequency controller all exclude it. Boundary control is a redesign option for a lower power or a higher VoV_oVo​, where the switching loss at VoV_oVo​ dominates. It needs a core rated for twice the input current, a controller with a zero-current detector, and an input filter for a 100 % triangle.

10.2 Discontinuous conduction with diode emulation

With MODE tied low, the LM5122 runs diode emulation: it turns Q1 off when the current reaches zero instead of letting it reverse (SNVS954H). Below the boundary the current then rests at zero until the next pulse. The cycle has three intervals: low side on (DDD), Q1 on (D2D_2D2​), and both off. Appendix D.2 derives the gain. The boundary load at each input follows from (D.1a):

(ours). The boundary is highest, 1.74 A, at 36 V, where DDD = 1/3 and KcritK_{\mathrm{crit}}Kcrit​ is at its 4/27 maximum. These are the same loads as the ZVS boundary of (6.3a). Below them forced PWM gives a negative valley, and diode emulation gives DCM.

At 36 V the DCM duty is less than half the CCM duty. At 48 V it is 0.055, and the 0.036 minimum of §10.4 is close. At light load and high input the minimum on-time comes close.

What the real controller adds. The LM5122 detects the zero crossing with a 6–7 mV threshold across the 4 mΩ shunt, that is at 1.5–1.75 A (ours, from SNVS954H). Q1 therefore stops early, and its body diode carries the tail of the current down to zero. The DCM of the board would be a diode-assisted DCM, with the body-diode drop on the tail.

At light load. Keep forced PWM to reproduce TI’s board. Its benefit is a fixed frequency, a fixed loop and partial or full ZVS of the low side at light load (§6.2). Its cost is the circulating current at light load, where the fixed snubber and output-charge losses already dominate (§8.4). Choose diode emulation if light-load efficiency matters more than a constant spectrum and loop. Then compare the DCM losses with a DCM model, because the CCM calculation cannot decide the DCM case.

10.4 Bypass at V_in ≥ V_o, and why the board stops at 50 V

As the input rises toward 54 V, the duty falls toward zero. The minimum low-side on-time, 158 ns with R14 = 30 kΩ (ours, from SNVS954H), sets the lowest duty, 0.036 at 230 kHz. Regulation therefore ends below VoV_oVo​:

  • No regulation. Above 54 V the output is the input. The 80 V MOSFETs and capacitors see whatever the source delivers.
  • No current limit. The cycle-by-cycle limit ends the low-side pulse. In bypass there is no low-side pulse, so nothing limits the current. A boost has no series switch to open: a short at the output draws the source current through L1 and Q1’s channel or body diode.
  • A saturated loop. The error amplifier sees the output too high and drives COMP low. When the input falls back below 52 V, the loop must recover from saturation, with an overshoot or a dip.

These are reasons to stop the tested range at 50 V, 4 V below VoV_oVo​ (TI). Decision. Use bypass only where the source cannot exceed the parts’ rating and an upstream fuse or eFuse limits the fault current. Otherwise a buck-boost, or a boost with an input disconnect switch, is the credible alternative.
