Appendix D — Critical and discontinuous conduction

D.1 Critical conduction: the boundary

Reduce the load current and the inductor valley falls. At a critical point the valley just reaches zero. Below that load, the diode blocks the negative current and the current “gaps” (German: lücken). The boundary is:

ΔIL2=Iin(D.1a)\frac{\Delta I_L}{2}=I_{\mathrm{in}}\tag{D.1a}2ΔIL​​=Iin​(D.1a)

Insert ΔIL=VoD(1−D)/(Lfs)\Delta I_L = V_oD(1-D)/(Lf_s)ΔIL​=Vo​D(1−D)/(Lfs​) and Iin=Vo/((1−D)Rcrit)I_{\mathrm{in}} = V_o/((1-D)R_{\mathrm{crit}})Iin​=Vo​/((1−D)Rcrit​):

VoD(1−D)2Lfs=Vo(1−D)Rcrit⇒2LfsRcrit=D(1−D)2(D.2a)\frac{V_oD(1-D)}{2Lf_s}=\frac{V_o}{(1-D)R_{\mathrm{crit}}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{2Lf_s}{R_{\mathrm{crit}}}=D(1-D)^2\tag{D.2a}2Lfs​Vo​D(1−D)​=(1−D)Rcrit​Vo​​⇒Rcrit​2Lfs​​=D(1−D)2(D.2a)

K=2LfsR,Kcrit(D)=D(1−D)2(D.3a)K=\frac{2Lf_s}{R},\qquad K_{\mathrm{crit}}(D)=D(1-D)^2\tag{D.3a}K=R2Lfs​​,Kcrit​(D)=D(1−D)2(D.3a)

As in the buck, the diode prevents a negative current. The period now has three intervals:

In DCM the gain rises above the CCM curve and depends on the load through KKK. A lighter load at the same duty gives a higher output. The controller must reduce DDD as the load falls. With no load at all, a diode boost at fixed duty would keep charging its output. Real controllers skip pulses.
