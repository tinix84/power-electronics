3. The inductor

3.1 The ripple ratio sets L, and the worst case sits in the middle

Integrate the low-side ramp of §2.1 over DTsDT_sDTs​ and substitute D=1−Vin/VoD = 1 - V_{\mathrm{in}}/V_oD=1−Vin​/Vo​. The result is equation (B.2a) of Appendix B.2: ΔIL=Vin D/(L fs)=Vin(1−Vin/Vo)/(L fs)\Delta I_L = V_{\mathrm{in}}\,D/(L\,f_s) = V_{\mathrm{in}}(1 - V_{\mathrm{in}}/V_o)/(L\,f_s)ΔIL​=Vin​D/(Lfs​)=Vin​(1−Vin​/Vo​)/(Lfs​).

At fixed VoV_oVo​ this is a parabola in VinV_{\mathrm{in}}Vin​. It is zero at both VinV_{\mathrm{in}}Vin​ = 0 and Vin=VoV_{\mathrm{in}} = V_oVin​=Vo​. The ripple peaks at Vin=Vo/2V_{\mathrm{in}} = V_o/2Vin​=Vo​/2, inside the range, not at an end. There DDD = 0.5:

ΔIL,max⁡=Vo4 L fsatVin=Vo2(3.1a)\Delta I_{L,\max}=\frac{V_o}{4\,L\,f_s}\qquad\mathrm{at}\qquad V_{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{V_o}{2}\tag{3.1a}ΔIL,max​=4Lfs​Vo​​atVin​=2Vo​​(3.1a)

ΔIL,max⁡=54 V4⋅10 μH⋅230 kHz=5.87 Aat27 V(3.1b)\Delta I_{L,\max}=\frac{54\ \mathrm{V}}{4\cdot10\ \mu\mathrm{H}\cdot230\ \mathrm{kHz}}=5.87\ \mathrm{A}\qquad\mathrm{at}\qquad27\ \mathrm{V}\tag{3.1b}ΔIL,max​=4⋅10 μH⋅230 kHz54 V​=5.87 Aat27 V(3.1b)

This is the dual of Lesson 1’s shortcut warning. There, VinV_{\mathrm{in}}Vin​ swept with VoV_oVo​ fixed and the ripple grew to the range end. Here the parabola turns inside the range. Find the worst case from the equation, not from habit.

State the ripple against the input current at the same input. The input current falls as 1/Vin1/V_{\mathrm{in}}1/Vin​ while the ripple first rises. The same 10 µH gives very different ratios (ours, TI’s 10 µH, design-checks.json):

The ratio exceeds 1 from 24 V to beyond 36 V. A ratio above 1 is not discontinuous conduction. The valley stays positive because the mean is still larger than half the ripple. The lowest full-load valley is 1.10 A at 38.4 V (ours), not at 27 V, because the input current falls faster than the ripple there. Full load therefore stays in CCM over the whole 12–50 V range.

We ask for a ripple of rrr = 0.30 of the full-load input current at 12 V, where the current is largest. Solving (B.2a) for L:

Our inductor is 12.0 µH (ours). Why specify at 12 V and not at 27 V? At 27 V the ripple is largest in amperes, but the peak current is smaller (7.93 A against 13.28 A). The peak and the conduction loss belong to 12 V. Always say at which input the ripple ratio is stated, and against which current.

3.2 Peak and RMS current with TI’s 10 µH

TI chose 10 µH, below our 12.0 µH. At 12 V it ripples by 4.06 A (ours, (B.2b)), a ratio of 0.36 instead of 0.30. Peak and valley follow:

Ipk=Iin+ΔIL2,Iv=Iin−ΔIL2(3.3a)I_{\mathrm{pk}}=I_{\mathrm{in}}+\frac{\Delta I_L}{2},\qquad I_v=I_{\mathrm{in}}-\frac{\Delta I_L}{2}\tag{3.3a}Ipk​=Iin​+2ΔIL​​,Iv​=Iin​−2ΔIL​​(3.3a)

Ipk=11.25+4.062=13.28 A,Iv=11.25−4.062=9.22 A(3.3b)I_{\mathrm{pk}}=11.25+\frac{4.06}{2}=13.28\ \mathrm{A},\qquad I_v=11.25-\frac{4.06}{2}=9.22\ \mathrm{A}\tag{3.3b}Ipk​=11.25+24.06​=13.28 A,Iv​=11.25−24.06​=9.22 A(3.3b)

The RMS current is 11.31 A (ours, (B.6b)), only 0.5 % above the 11.25 A mean. At this ripple ratio the DC term dominates every conduction loss, as in the buck. Which current measure enters a loss depends on the element. A constant voltage takes the mean, a resistance takes the RMS (Lesson 1 §3.2). Never use the mean current for a resistive loss.

3.3 Checks against the real part

  • Saturation. Coilcraft specifies a 10 % drop of inductance at 18.0 A, 20 % at 21.5 A and 30 % at 23.4 A (TI’s part, Coilcraft Doc 644-1). Our 13.28 A peak at 12 V and full load leaves 4.72 A of margin (ours). It passes.
  • Hard saturation. The SER2915H is a ferrite E core, not a metal composite like Lesson 1’s Würth part. A gapped ferrite keeps its inductance flat and then loses it within a small increase of current. The current slope vL/Lv_L/LvL​/L then rises steeply, and the current can run away within a few cycles. With a ferrite core the rule “peak below Isat” is strict.
  • Current limit: No! The LM5122 ends the low-side pulse when 75 mV appear across R2. With 4 mΩ that is 75 mV/4 mΩ = 18.75 A (ours, from SNVS954H Sec. 7.3.10). This lies above the 18.0 A at which the core has lost 10 %. In an overload or a start-up into a short, the core saturates before the limit acts. Only the 65.5 mV minimum of the threshold, 16.4 A, lies below Isat (ours). Always check the inductor at the current limit, not only at full load. To fix it, raise R2 so that the limit falls below Isat. The shunt loss of §8.1 then rises in proportion. Or choose a core with a higher Isat.
  • Heating. Coilcraft rates the part at 20 A RMS for a 20 K rise and 30 A for 40 K (TI’s part). At 11.31 A RMS it runs well inside. The DCR is 1.86 mΩ typical and 2.05 mΩ maximum.
  • Self-resonance. The 15 MHz self-resonant frequency is far above the 230 kHz switching frequency and its first harmonics (TI’s part).
  • Tolerance. Check the ripple and the peak at the low end of the inductance tolerance, and with the inductance the core still has at the peak current. With a hard-saturating core the second effect is small below Isat and large above it.
  • Slew rate. A larger L lowers the ripple but also the current slew rate Vin/LV_{\mathrm{in}}/LVin​/L that the controller can command. In a boost the RHP zero (§7) already limits the loop. A slower current makes it worse.

Why the inductor sits on the input side of the shunt. TI places R2 between the input and L1 (TIDRTY1). The shunt therefore sees the inductor current continuously, not only during the on-time. The LM5122 can sense the peak, the valley and the zero crossing (§10.2) with one resistor. The cost is the loss of §8.1: 0.55 W at 12 V (ours), because the shunt carries IinI_{\mathrm{in}}Iin​ all the time.

TI chose, TI measured. TI chose a 10 µH Coilcraft SER2915H-103KL, below our 12.0 µH. The ripple at 12 V is therefore 0.36 of the input current, a little above our 0.30. TI did not measure the inductor current. §9 checks the ripple it causes at the output. From here on the lesson continues with TI’s 10 µH, and with the current-limit failure on the page.

3.4 What if we allow 10 % or 100 % current ripple?

Our 30 % target is a design choice. To see its consequences, compare two extremes. Here ripple means peak-to-peak inductor current divided by the 11.25 A full-load input current at 12 V. It is not duty ratio. Keep 12 V input, 54 V output and 230 kHz fixed. Use the ideal CCM equations of §3.1–3.2.

Before calculating, predict which choice needs more inductance. Which capacitor gets more work, the input or the output one?

Quantity at 12 V, full load 10 % ripple 30 % (ours) 100 % ripple
Peak-to-peak inductor ripple 1.125 A 3.375 A 11.25 A
Required ideal inductance 36.07 µH 12.02 µH 3.607 µH
Valley / peak inductor current 10.69 / 11.81 A 9.56 / 12.94 A 5.63 / 16.88 A
Inductor RMS current 11.255 A 11.292 A 11.709 A

(ours, design-checks.json, inductor.what_if_ratio)

In a boost, the inductor ripple is the input ripple. The triangle of §2.3 flows into the input bank. Its RMS value, ΔIL/12\Delta I_L/\sqrt{12}ΔIL​/12​, grows ten times from the 10 % to the 100 % design. The output capacitor barely notices. Its current is the pulse of IinI_{\mathrm{in}}Iin​, and the pulse depends on the mean, not on the ripple.

Choose 10 % when input-ripple and peak-current stress matter. The input bank and the EMC filter see a small triangle, and the peak stays near the mean. The cost is three times the inductance of our design, a larger part, and a current that changes three times more slowly.

Choose 100 % when a small inductor justifies the ripple. The inductance falls to 3.607 µH. The peak reaches 16.88 A, and the new part must be rated for it with margin. The input bank must take ten times the ripple current. This remains CCM at full load: the valley is still 5.63 A. At light load it moves into DCM or into negative current much sooner (§10.2).

Is low ripple always more efficient? No. It depends on which loss dominates. Low ripple lowers the core loss and the input-capacitor RMS current. It barely changes the conduction loss, because IL,rmsI_{L,\mathrm{rms}}IL,rms​ stays close to IinI_{\mathrm{in}}Iin​ (11.255 A against 11.709 A). In a boost, lower ripple also raises the valley. A higher valley raises the turn-on overlap loss at VoV_oVo​ (§8.1). In industry the final choice is usually cost, weighed against efficiency and volume.

Our decision. Keep 30 % at 12 V as the target and continue with TI’s 10 µH, which gives 36 %. The benefit is a moderate peak, 13.28 A, inside a strict ferrite Isat.
