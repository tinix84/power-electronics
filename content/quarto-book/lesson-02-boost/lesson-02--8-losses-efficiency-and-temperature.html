8. Losses, efficiency and temperature

8.1 Where the losses come from: the datasheet estimate

The ideal boost of §2.3 is lossless. The losses come from the components, not from the conversion principle. We first estimate every loss from datasheets only: BSC072N08NS5 (Infineon), LM5122 (SNVS954H) and Coilcraft Doc 644-1, with no measured number (ti-pmp21274/scripts/loss_estimate.py). The capacitor ESR terms, below 0.03 W, are left out of the rows. Three terms do not change at all: output charge, reverse recovery and snubber, 1.32 W together at every input. They depend on VoV_oVo​ and fsf_sfs​ only, as long as the low side turns on hard. The core loss follows the ripple of §3.1 and peaks in the middle of the range. The overlap rises again at 48 V because the valley current rises there (§3.1).

Why a high boost ratio increases loss in this model. In the lecture of 2026-10-30, I contrasted boost and buck efficiency. The following calculation explains this boost’s loss mechanisms. It does not establish a topology ranking against another board. At 12 V the low side carries IinI_{\mathrm{in}}Iin​ = 11.68 A, 4.7 times the output current, for DDD = 0.781 of the period. Every low-side turn-on happens at VoV_oVo​ = 54 V, the higher of the two voltages. Conduction costs 1.68 W and switching at VoV_oVo​ costs 3.08 W of the 5.19 W (ours). For a fixed output, the ideal mean input current grows as Io/(1−D)I_o/(1-D)Io​/(1−D). With fixed resistance, its conduction loss therefore grows with the square of this current. The overlap estimate also grows with the positive valley current at fixed VoV_oVo​ and fsf_sfs​. Appendix C.2 shows the corresponding gain peak and efficiency decrease for the slide example. A buck comparison needs matched operating conditions and component assumptions (§1).

What would we improve first? At 12 V, find the 14.9 mΩ: measure the drop from the input connector to the switch node at full load. A lower-resistance layout attacks the largest fitted term. Then the overlap: a stronger low-side gate drive shortens the 12.6 ns, but it raises the ringing that the snubber must damp. At 48 V the fixed terms dominate: snubber, output charge and gate drive. There a smaller snubber capacitor or a GaN stage helps more. Let the intended operating range decide which comes first.
